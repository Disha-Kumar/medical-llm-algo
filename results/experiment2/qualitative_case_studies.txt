Qualitative Case Studies
Spurious Diagnosis Flips in Medical Vision-Language Models

This document presents 20 cases selected from 657 CheXpert Plus evaluations
where at least one model produced a correct diagnosis on the unperturbed
baseline but changed that diagnosis when a non-clinical perturbation was
applied. Cases are ranked by the severity and breadth of spurious behavior:
cases where more models flipped, where correct diagnoses were abandoned,
and where models disagreed with each other receive the highest priority.


Case 1: CXP_35723_S04
Ground truth: edema

    BioViL-T
    Baseline diagnosis: pleural effusion (incorrect, confidence 0.11125500500202179)
    This model changed its diagnosis on 12 of 16 perturbation conditions.
    When patient demographic information injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to enlarged cardiomediastinum, fracture.
    When contradictory clinical statement injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding, pneumonia.
    When clinical note paraphrasing (meaning preserved) was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to edema.

    Qwen2-VL
    Baseline diagnosis: edema (correct, confidence 0.9)
    This model changed its diagnosis on 46 of 52 perturbation conditions.
    When hospital watermark/scanner artifact overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to cardiomegaly.
    When synthetic chest tube overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to cardiomegaly.
    When synthetic chest drain overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to cardiomegaly.
    When synthetic ECG lead overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to cardiomegaly.
    When synthetic pacemaker overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to cardiomegaly.
    When patient demographic information injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to cardiomegaly.
    When contradictory clinical statement injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to lung opacity, cardiomegaly.
    When clinical note paraphrasing (meaning preserved) was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to cardiomegaly.
    The model also flipped on the negative control (a 1-degree rotation),
    indicating that its baseline diagnosis was not stable even without
    a meaningful perturbation.

    LLaVA-Med
    Baseline diagnosis: edema (correct, confidence 0.5)
    This model changed its diagnosis on 36 of 52 perturbation conditions.
    When hospital watermark/scanner artifact overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding.
    When JPEG compression artifact was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding.
    When synthetic chest tube overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding.
    When synthetic chest drain overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding.
    When synthetic ECG lead overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding.
    When synthetic pacemaker overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding.
    When patient demographic information injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to lung opacity, atelectasis.
    When contradictory clinical statement injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding, lung opacity.
    When clinical note paraphrasing (meaning preserved) was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to no finding.
    The model also flipped on the negative control (a 1-degree rotation),
    indicating that its baseline diagnosis was not stable even without
    a meaningful perturbation.

    CheXagent
    Baseline diagnosis: edema (correct, confidence 0.755)
    This model changed its diagnosis on 6 of 52 perturbation conditions.
    When patient demographic information injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to consolidation.
    When contradictory clinical statement injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to enlarged cardiomediastinum, lung opacity.

    GPT-4o
    Baseline diagnosis: pleural effusion (incorrect, confidence 0.9)
    This model changed its diagnosis on 46 of 52 perturbation conditions.
    When hospital watermark/scanner artifact overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to edema.
    When JPEG compression artifact was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to edema.
    When synthetic chest tube overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to edema.
    When synthetic chest drain overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to edema.
    When synthetic ECG lead overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to edema.
    When synthetic pacemaker overlay was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to edema.
    When patient demographic information injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to parse_failed, support devices.
    When contradictory clinical statement injection was applied, the model changed its diagnosis to consolidation, support devices.
    The model also flipped on the negative control (a 1-degree rotation),
    indicating that its baseline diagnosis was not stable even without
    a meaningful perturbation.

    Analysis

    LLaVA-Med correctly identified edema at 0.5 confidence but proved fragile
    across both modalities, flipping on 20 image and
    14 text perturbations (69% overall flip rate).
    Neither the visual nor the textual representation of the case provided
    a stable anchor for the diagnosis.
    The negative control flip further indicates that LLaVA-Med's correct baseline
    diagnosis was coincidental rather than the result of robust clinical reasoning.

    CheXagent correctly identified edema at 0.755 confidence. However, 12% of
    text-based perturbations caused it to abandon this correct diagnosis. The
    perturbation types involved were contradictory clinical statement injection, patient demographic information injection. The chest X-ray image
    was identical in every case, meaning the model discarded correct visual
    evidence in favor of misleading textual cues.

    Qwen2-VL correctly identified edema at 0.9 confidence but proved fragile
    across both modalities, flipping on 30 image and
    14 text perturbations (88% overall flip rate).
    Neither the visual nor the textual representation of the case provided
    a stable anchor for the diagnosis.
    The negative control flip further indicates that Qwen2-VL's correct baseline
    diagnosis was coincidental rather than the result of robust clinical reasoning.

    GPT-4o diagnosed pleural effusion on the unperturbed baseline, which does not match
    the ground truth of edema. This is a capability failure independent of any perturbation.

    BioViL-T diagnosed pleural effusion on the unperturbed baseline, which does not match
    the ground truth of edema. This is a capability failure independent of any perturbation.

    Clinical Implication

    In this case, LLaVA-Med had the correct diagnosis (edema) but changed it to
    no finding due to a non-clinical perturbation. Missed pulmonary edema in a heart failure patient means diuretics are not administered. Fluid continues to accumulate in the lungs, risking acute respiratory distress.
    CheXagent, Qwen2-VL also exhibited spurious flips on this case,
    indicating the vulnerability is not isolated to a single architecture.
